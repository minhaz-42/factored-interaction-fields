\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Interaction fields}
ARCTIC~\cite{fan2023arctic} introduced interaction-field estimation as the dense, per-vertex distance from one mesh to the closest vertex of another, and proposed InterField, a PointNet regressor over canonical-pose vertices concatenated with a global image feature, in single-frame and recurrent variants. TACO~\cite{liu2024taco} extended the benchmark to tool-object fields. SHOW3D~\cite{rim2026show3d} re-used the scalar formulation to study transfer between in-the-wild and studio data, and its challenge starter kit~\cite{rim2026show3dapi} defined the joint-anchored three-dimensional vector field of \cref{eq:field} together with an image-only ResNet-50~\cite{he2016resnet} baseline. The published challenge solutions share one architecture: DINOv3 tokens from both headset views decoded by 42 joint queries into vectors trained with the endpoint error. GOLF~\cite{zou2026golf} adapts a ViT-H+ with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora}, samples region tokens around localised hands and the object, and tags tokens with Pl\"ucker rays; JSSR~\cite{jin2026jssr} adds temporal context, an epipolar candidate search for the endpoints and a learned residual gate. None of them uses the object mesh or predicts the quantities that define the field; GOLF uses the object category only to condition its region locator, and JSSR deliberately avoids CAD models. Our work is complementary to backbone adaptation and region sampling and targets the missing structure.

\subsection{Contact and proximity from posed geometry}
Contact maps, potential fields and signed distances are routinely derived from posed hand and object meshes. CPF~\cite{yang2021cpf} models contacting vertex pairs as springs, ContactOpt~\cite{grady2021contactopt} refines grasps towards a predicted contact map, DECO~\cite{tripathi2023deco} predicts dense body-scene contact, HOISDF~\cite{qi2024hoisdf} and gSDF~\cite{chen2023gsdf} constrain joint hand-object pose regression with signed distance fields, and EgoPHI~\cite{ilic2026egophi} estimates dense contact and force on meshes from a single egocentric image given the object geometry. Datasets such as ContactPose~\cite{brahmbhatt2020contactpose} and GRAB~\cite{taheri2020grab} record contact as a consequence of pose. These methods treat proximity as derived from geometry; the interaction field is exactly such a derived quantity, and our geometric head makes the dependence explicit and differentiable. A recent survey of hand-object interaction with foundation models~\cite{lin2026hoisurvey} distinguishes contact maps, affordance maps, field representations and dense correspondences, and flags the gap between geometric completeness and interaction correctness that motivates our decomposition.

\subsection{Egocentric hand and hand-object pose}
Egocentric benchmarks with three-dimensional hand and object annotations have grown from single-view RGB-D~\cite{garcia2018fpha} through multi-camera studio capture~\cite{hampali2020honnotate,chao2021dexycb,kwon2021h2o,liu2022hoi4d} to headset capture at scale~\cite{ohkawa2023assemblyhands,banerjee2025hot3d,grauman2024egoexo4d,rim2026show3d}. Hand-object reconstruction from a single image was addressed by ObMan~\cite{hasson2019obman}; large vision transformers established strong single-hand mesh recovery~\cite{pavlakos2024hamer}; recent egocentric hand-object pose estimators such as HOPformer~\cite{bansal2026hopformer} and EgoGrasp~\cite{fu2026egograsp}, and the HANDS~2023 challenge analysis~\cite{fan2024benchmarks}, report that close contact, narrow object views and fast motion remain the hard cases. Most of these datasets use the MANO hand model~\cite{romero2017embodied}, whose joint convention differs from the UmeTrack landmarks of SHOW3D and HOT3D by several millimetres; we therefore restrict cross-dataset experiments to HOT3D, the only public dataset that shares the skeleton, the meshes and the headset.

\subsection{Object pose}
Our geometric head recovers the object pose in closed form from predicted control points by weighted Procrustes alignment~\cite{kabsch1976solution,arun1987least,umeyama1991least} and is trained with the symmetry-agnostic ADD-S distance of PoseCNN~\cite{xiang2018posecnn}, the mean distance from each posed model point to the closest point of the ground-truth posed model, which the BOP benchmark adopted~\cite{hodan2020bop}. SHOW3D's own object ground truth was produced with FoundPose~\cite{ornek2024foundpose} followed by tracking, with reported translation errors of 1.5 to 3.5\,mm~\cite{rim2026show3d}, a noise floor relevant to our oracle analysis.

\subsection{Multi-view geometry in networks}
Epipolar Transformers~\cite{he2020epipolar} and learnable triangulation~\cite{iskakov2019learnable} fuse features across calibrated views for human pose; geometry-biased attention~\cite{moliner2024geometry} is a related alternative; UmeTrack~\cite{han2022umetrack} is the multi-view, world-space egocentric hand tracker whose skeleton SHOW3D and HOT3D adopt. Ray embeddings introduced for neural rendering~\cite{sitzmann2021lfn} and view synthesis~\cite{jin2025lvsm} let a transformer reason about camera geometry without explicit epipolar operations; GOLF and our baselines use them. The challenge reports found stereo fusion to add little to direct regression~\cite{zou2026golf,jin2026jssr}; our decomposition of the endpoint error into a component along the viewing ray and a lateral remainder examines why.

\subsection{Soft nearest neighbours and differentiable geometry}
Replacing a hard argmin by a temperature-controlled softmax is a standard device for making discrete geometric operations differentiable, used for disparity regression~\cite{kendall2017gcnet} and for neural nearest-neighbour selection~\cite{plotz2018n3net}. Our soft nearest-vertex operator applies the same idea to the nearest-surface-point map and anneals the temperature so that the exact operator is recovered at test time.

\subsection{Uncertainty and fusion}
Heteroscedastic regression, in which a network predicts the variance of its own residual and is trained with a Gaussian negative log-likelihood, dates back to Nix and Weigend~\cite{nix1994estimating} and was brought to computer vision by Kendall and Gal~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties}; its calibration is known to be imperfect~\cite{guo2017calibration}, and aleatoric heads have been shown to improve three-dimensional hand pose~\cite{lee2025correlation}. Combining several estimators by their predicted precisions is the maximum-likelihood rule for independent Gaussians and is closely related to mixtures of experts~\cite{jacobs1991adaptive} and deep ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}. We use per-joint heteroscedastic scales both as a training objective and as the fusion rule between two estimators of the same field, and we report how the learned scales behaved in practice, including a run in which one head's scale saturated.

\subsection{Transfer between datasets}
That models fit the peculiarities of the dataset they were trained on has been documented since Torralba and Efros~\cite{torralba2011unbiased}; SHOW3D itself reports scalar-field transfer between studio and in-the-wild data~\cite{rim2026show3d}. We add a joint-level transfer protocol between SHOW3D and HOT3D and test whether geometry-factored predictors transfer better than appearance-driven regressors.
